# Argonisos performance review (20 November 2026)

> Kept from the review round of 20 November 2026. The screenshots, probe scripts and raw logs it names were in the
> reviewer's scratch folder and are not kept; the line numbers are as of the commit it names.

Machine: Apple M4, 10 cores, headless Chrome with ANGLE on Metal, 1280×720, resolution pinned at 100% (as the perf suite),
High preset unless stated. Five other reviewers were running headless browsers on the same GPU during this session, so
every figure below carries the 1-minute load average when it was taken. Wherever load could distort a number I used
counts, CPU profiles, allocation profiles, or A/B runs taken back to back. Code is at `7e54596`/`fd30a4c`. No file
under `src/` or `tests/` was edited: fixes were tried by patching the served file in the probe (`PATCH` in
`probe/lib.mjs`).

Probe scripts and raw output: `scratchpad/review/probe/` (`lib.mjs`, `forest.mjs`, `alloc.mjs`, `gpu.mjs`, `ab.mjs`,
`measure.mjs`, `bakecost.mjs`, `programs.mjs`, `reeval.mjs`, `version.mjs`, `leak.mjs`, `leakplay.mjs`, `heap.mjs`,
`snapshot.mjs`/`snapsum.mjs`, `out/`).

## 0. The forest regression: what it is and what it isn't

There are two separate things.

**(a) A new hitch, introduced by `7e54596` today. This is a real code regression.** Every bake of the sky's light now
links a shader program again. In the woods, each bake costs 54–57 ms of main-thread time, so you get a 60 ms frame.
- Evidence, from `bakecost.mjs` with the day running at normal pace for 40 s in the forest, all three runs back to back:
  - HEAD: 7 bake frames. Their `sky, weather` lap was 54.6, 56.1, 55.5, 56.7, 55.5, 56.0 and 53.8 ms, and each one
    linked exactly one program. Load 3.5.
  - `aa43258` (the commit before `7e54596`): no frame over 4 ms. Load 4.0.
  - HEAD with the one-line fix below: no frame over 4 ms. Load 3.9.
- How often it happens: 16 links a minute at normal pace in the meadow. Under time ×60 (T, or sleeping) it is
  **224 a minute**. All of them come from `refreshEnvironment` (`sky.js:100`). Load 3.3.
- With time frozen (as the perf suite runs) it still happens every 6.25 s, through the `envTimer < -6` rule at
  `sky.js:165`.
- In the meadow the same bakes cost under 4 ms. They are only expensive where the GPU is already the bottleneck. The
  link ends in synchronous program queries (`getProgramInfoLog` and `getUniforms` in three's `onFirstUse`). These make
  the main thread wait until the GPU process has worked through its backlog, and in the forest that backlog is a whole
  frame or more. The CPU profile shows the same thing: `getProgramInfoLog` under `refreshEnvironment` took 1.7% of all
  samples.
- The bot playtest's "275 hitches over 50 ms" (timewarp at 43 fps average, the boss fights and the woods) is consistent
  with this hitch at 16 to 224 a minute.
- **Cause.**
  - The audit's fix (`sky.js:97-101`) keeps a copy of three's PMREM background box (`keepBg`) so that its program stays
    alive. `keepBg` is drawn once, at the first bake, with the main camera, and it stands at the world origin.
  - Until `7e54596`, the camera started the first frame at the origin and eased towards the hero, so the box was in
    view and its program was made.
  - `player.js:296` (`if (jump) camera.position.copy(want)`) now puts the camera at the hero at once. The box is
    frustum-culled, nothing is drawn, no program is kept, and every `pmrem.fromScene` compiles and links
    `PMREM.Background` again and then frees it the same frame.
- **Proof.** The cache key of the bake's program matched no live program. A box drawn with `frustumCulled = false`
  produced exactly the key PMREM wants, and after that the bakes linked nothing (`keepbg.mjs`, `progpush.mjs`).
- **Fix (S, no risk).** In `sky.js:98`, set `keepBg.frustumCulled = false`. Better still, draw it with a camera of its
  own, so it doesn't depend on where the player stands. Also add a check to `tests/suites/loop.mjs` or `perf.mjs` that
  no program is linked over 10 s with the day running.
- **Gain.** Removes every bake hitch. In the A/B of HEAD against the fixed version (3 rounds each in the forest, load
  3.3–4.9):
  - frames over 50 ms in 20 s: 4–5 → 1;
  - worst JS frame: 60–72 ms → 11.7–12.5 ms.

**(b) The perf suite's forest result (54.9 fps, p95 33.3 ms) is the M4's GPU at its limit. It is not a code
regression.**
- **The GPU is the limit.** Taken back to back at load 3.4–3.9:
  - In the forest, the GPU process's main thread is 96% busy, and 77% of its time is spent in
    `CALayerTreeCoordinator::ApplyBackpressure::Metal`, waiting for Metal to finish earlier frames.
  - In the meadow, the same thread is 17% busy with no back-pressure.
  - The WebGL command decoding costs the same in both (132–150 ms/s).
  - The game's own code takes 4.4 ms a frame in the forest (p95 6.1 ms) and 3.7 ms in the meadow.
  - The middle frame in the forest is 17.3 ms, just over one refresh, so a large share of frames miss vsync and take
    33 ms (`forest.mjs`, `gputhread.mjs`).
- **The fps doesn't depend on the version.** I A/B'd seven commits in the forest from worktrees with the pack symlinked
  in (`ab.mjs`): `02d9228` (Patch 19), `f1a4d23`, `dc41d8c` (Patch 20), `1886eb6`, `e379aed`, `aa43258` and HEAD. Every
  one landed between 46 and 60 fps depending on the load at the moment (for example, `02d9228` gave 60.2 at load 4.4
  and 50.2 at load 7.6).
  - The perf history agrees. The forest has varied between 50 and 60 fps since 13 November with identical draw counts:
    60.1 at 15:08 and 50.8 at 16:10, both with 3.22M triangles. The audit itself recorded the High forest at 50–51 fps
    on this Mac.
- **The one deterministic change.** Patch 20 (`dc41d8c`: trees 30% taller) raised the forest from 3.19M to 3.40M
  triangles and added leaf overdraw, which moved the forest's typical result from about 60 to about 57.
- **Any modest GPU saving brings p95 back to 16.8 ms.** Each line below is one thing turned off; the load during the run
  was about 5, and the baseline was 49.9 fps with p95 33.3 ms (`gpu.mjs`):

  | Turned off | fps | p95 (ms) |
  |---|---|---|
  | grass | 55.3 | 16.8 |
  | shadow maps | 54.9 | 16.8 |
  | vegetation | 54.9 | 16.8 |
  | resolution at 70% | 54.0 | 16.8 |
  | MSAA | 54.0 | 16.8 |
  | terrain | 52.8 | 16.8 |
  | atmosphere | 52.4 | 16.8 |
  | bloom | 52.1 | 16.8 |

  So the forest needs about 10–15% less GPU work to hold 60 on this Mac. Items 3, 4, 7 and 11 below are where that can
  come from without changing the picture.
- **Patch 22's suspects are all near zero** (wrapped per-frame cost in the forest, load 3.5):

  | Suspect | Cost per frame |
  |---|---|
  | `harvest.update` (the tree and log physics; logs at rest are skipped) | 0.002–0.005 ms |
  | `build.update` | 0.004 ms |
  | `trial.update` (the storm) | 0.003–0.006 ms |
  | `creatures.update` | 0.005 ms |

  - The `veg.onCell` wrapper in `build.js:97` runs only when a cell arrives, not every frame.
  - GC is minor: 0.5 minor GCs a second at 1–2 ms each, and no major GC in a 6 s trace.

## Ranked findings

Format: issue · evidence (load) · cause (file:line) · fix · expected gain · risk · effort.

### 1. Every sky bake links a program again: 55–60 ms hitches in GPU-bound places (the forest, Ultra), up to 224 a minute under fast time
Covered fully in §0(a). Cause: `sky.js:98` with `player.js:296`. Fix: `keepBg.frustumCulled = false`. Gain: no bake hitches
(worst JS frame 60–72 → 12 ms). Risk none. **S.**
- **On Ultra it is worse.** The worst frame in the forest was 116–130 ms and `sky, weather` peaked at 116 ms (load 3.6).
- **A broader guard.** Three's own shader check, `renderer.debug.checkShaderErrors`, adds synchronous queries on every
  link. It could be switched off outside dev and the tests (`?debug`); this is low risk, because the warm-up already
  compiles everything.

### 2. A transparent double-sided material forces two draws and two program look-ups per mesh, every frame (sites' fires)
- **Evidence.**
  - In the forest, one `MeshBasicMaterial` (white, `map`, transparent, double-sided, 35 meshes in a site group) had its
    program looked up 42 times a frame and its `version` bumped 504 times a second, all from three's `renderObject`
    two-pass path (`version.mjs`, `reeval.mjs`, load 4).
  - Its share of the garbage: `getParameters` 137–145 KB a frame, `getProgramCacheKeyParameters` 23 KB, `join` 36 KB.
    That is about 12 MB/s of the 36–41 MB/s total (`alloc.mjs`, load 3.5). `getParameters` is 1.5% of the main
    thread's self time.
- **Cause.** `structures.js:104` (`flame`). Three r160 draws every transparent `DoubleSide` material in two passes, back
  faces then front, setting `needsUpdate` before each one.
  - The same pattern appears in `glow()` at `structures.js:219`, `glowMat` at `fx.js:81`, the beams at `save.js:82` and
    `trial.js:83`, the clouds at `clouds.js:146` and `:247`, and the storm wall at `stormwall.js:207`.
- **Fix.** Add `forceSinglePass: true` to the additive ones: flame, glow, glowMat and the beams. They have no depth
  write, and additive blending doesn't depend on order, so the second pass gains nothing. Check the clouds and the storm
  wall by eye before changing them.
- **Gain.** Measured with the flame alone: 21 fewer draws in the forest (508 → 487), about 0.2–0.4 ms less JS a frame,
  and about 12 MB/s less garbage. Risk: very low for additive materials. **S.**

### 3. The grass draws its full 160k + 100k blades everywhere, even where none show (1.5M triangles, 45% of the forest's)
- **Evidence.**
  - The grass's instanced draws are 23 draws and 1.53M of the forest's 3.40M triangles on High. On Ultra they are 3.04M
    of 6.97M (`gpu.mjs`, load 4.3).
  - Grass off: 49.9 → 55.3 fps on High (load about 5) and 22.9 → 30.9 fps on Ultra (load 3.9).
  - In the meadow, the near carpet's 160k blades are all faded out (their place is taken by the clumps:
    `meadowShare` 1.0 within 37 m) but are still vertex-shaded, with two texture reads per vertex.
  - In the forest the grass amount is low (mean 0.38, against 0.90 in the meadow), and it only shortens blades. It
    never removes them (`misc.mjs`).
- **Cause.** `grass.js:99-103`. Blades are dropped in the vertex shader (`fade`, `sc = 0`), so every vertex of every
  instance is still processed. Each blade also carries a whole `instanceMatrix` (64 bytes) although the shader reads
  only `instanceMatrix[3].xyz`. That is 16.6 MB on the CPU, the same again on the GPU, and four vec4 fetches per vertex.
- **Fix (no picture change).**
  1. Set the near carpet's `count` to 0 (or hide it) when the meadow share is about 1 everywhere within `HAND[1]`
     (37 m). The worker already builds that texture.
  2. Replace `instanceMatrix` with a `vec2` offset attribute (`InstancedBufferAttribute`, 8 bytes a blade).
  3. Optionally, order the instances so that the `keep`/`r2` thinning becomes a prefix, then draw
     `count = DENSE * keepShare`.
- **Gain.** In meadows, most of the near carpet's vertex cost: about 1M triangles. In the forest, about 10–20% of the
  grass's vertex cost from the slimmer attribute, which is enough to clear the 10–15% this Mac needs. Also 16 MB less
  heap. Risk: low for (1) and (2), but check the screenshot suite. **M.**

### 4. Two major sites a kilometre away cost 172 draws and 0.49M triangles from inside the forest
- **Evidence.** From the forest place, the temple at 999 m (76 meshes, 417k triangles) and the labyrinth at 953 m
  (96 meshes, 69k triangles) are drawn in full behind the woods and the mist (`sites.mjs`, load 3.5). `Group(site)`
  accounts for 125 of the forest's 508 draws on High and 136 of 621 on Ultra.
- **Cause.** `structures.js:135`: `VIEW_R` temple 1700 and labyrinth 1400 (×1.25 on Ultra). The near model is drawn at
  any distance, and there is no lower level of detail.
- **Fix.** Past about 350 m, swap each major site for a merged far version: one mesh per material, built at load with
  `mergeByMat` (already in `structures.js:192`), or a simplified hull. On Ultra, draw calls cost more CPU through
  ANGLE on D3D11 than they do on Metal.
- **Gain.** About 150 fewer draws and about 0.4M fewer triangles wherever a big site is in view. That is 25–30% of the
  forest's draws, and part of the GPU time it needs. Risk: low to medium (a pop at the swap, hidden by the mist). **M.**

### 5. The occlusion fader ray-tests every near tree batch every 80 ms (1.7 ms spikes on High, 4.5 ms on Ultra)
- **Evidence.**
  - `occlusion.update` averaged 0.33 ms a frame on High in the forest (p95 of the `fx, lights` lap 1.7 ms) and 0.89 ms
    a frame on Ultra (p95 2.8 ms, load 3.6–5).
  - In the meadow it was 0.03 ms.
  - `batchRaycast` was 2.4% of main-thread time in the forest CPU profile.
- **Cause.** `occlusion.js:72-77`. `candidates()` gathers every batch mesh with instances (`out.push(...b.meshes)`, a
  new array each time) and `intersectObjects` sorts all the hits.
- **Fix.** Find the trees standing between the camera and the hero from the colliders' spatial grid. `colliders.near`
  is already used for the hugging test. Then test only those instances, or only the batches that hold them. Reuse the
  candidate array.
- **Gain.** The forest's 1.7 ms (High) and 4.5 ms (Ultra) spikes drop to well under 0.3 ms. Risk: low. Run the
  vegetation suite and the fade tests. **M.**

### 6. The mid band is rebuilt in full each time a far block arrives (up to 8.8 ms a frame on Ultra, 193 KB of garbage)
- **Evidence.** On Ultra, standing still in the forest several seconds after arriving, `veg.rebuildMid` averaged
  0.52 ms a frame, the `tree packing` lap reached p95 7.5 ms and max 8.8 ms, and `rebuildMid` allocated 193 KB a frame
  (load 5.1). On High it appears at about 0.06 calls a frame.
- **Cause.** `vegetation.js:333` sets `midDirty` for each block that comes in (and `:341` when one crosses `MID_R`).
  `rebuildMid` (`vegetation.js:404`) then re-packs every block.
- **Fix.** Coalesce the rebuilds: rebuild at most every 0.5 s while blocks are streaming, or append the new block's
  instances in place. This is also an open item in the audit.
- **Gain.** No 5–9 ms frames on Ultra while you travel or after a teleport; less GC. Risk: low. **M.**

### 7. Shadow maps: the near map re-draws every frame, and Ultra's far map re-draws 1.28M triangles every 8 m walked
- **Evidence.**
  - Shadow maps off: 49.9 → 54.9 fps on High in the forest (load about 5).
  - In the forest, the near map draws 43 vegetation draws (125k triangles) plus the fx and pickup instances.
  - On Ultra, a far-map redraw is 118 draws and 1.28M triangles (`gpu.mjs`).
- **Cause.** By design (`core.js:44-73`). `SHADOW.farStep` is 8 m on Ultra. Patch 20's taller trees enlarged both maps'
  casters.
- **Fix (no picture change).**
  - Spread the far map over two frames: half its batches each frame, into the same map, at the next redraw.
  - Cull the near map's stand-ins by the sun's frustum and the view together (not drawn when both their shadow and
    their trunk lie outside the camera's frustum).
  - On Ultra, consider `farStep` 12.
- **Gain.** About 1 ms of GPU time on High in the woods. Risk: low to medium (shadow pops if it's done wrong; check
  `light.mjs`). **M.**

### 8. Falling leaves look up the full terrain height for every leaf, every frame (forest only; 100 KB a frame of garbage)
- **Evidence.**
  - In the forest, `rawHeight` is the largest allocation outside three: 88–102 KB a frame, plus `fbm` 30 KB, `noise`
    15 KB, `detail` 15 KB and `peakHeight` 6 KB. That is about 9 MB/s together (`alloc.mjs`, load 3.5).
  - The CPU profile places `heightAt` under `groundAt` under `updateAmbient` at 0.43% of all samples.
  - The meadow has no leaves, so it doesn't pay this.
- **Cause.**
  - `ambient.js:65`: `y < groundAt(x, z)` for every leaf and snow particle, every frame.
  - `gen.js:214`: `rawHeight(x, z, w = biomeWeights(x, z))` makes a new weights object on every call.
- **Fix.** Store each particle's ground height when it respawns (`respawn`, `ambient.js:39`), since a leaf drifts only a
  few metres before it lands, or read the grass height texture. In `rawHeight`, take a reusable `_w` scratch object.
  Keep the world fingerprint unchanged and re-run `npm run test:world`.
- **Gain.** About 0.2 ms a frame and about 9 MB/s less garbage in the woods. Risk: low. **S.**

### 9. Felled trees and faded trees leave their instanced meshes undisposed: a slow GL buffer leak
- **Evidence.**
  - `leakplay.mjs` with the camera held still (load 3.6–5): 5 rounds of felling 3 trees each, spawning and removing
    every creature kind, 3 lightning strikes, 80 fx calls, and 5 drops taken.
  - Results:

    | Measure | Change per round | Accounted for by |
    |---|---|---|
    | geometries | +3 | logs (+3–4 a round, by design) |
    | scene objects | +6–8 | logs |
    | live GL buffers (created minus deleted) | +15–20 | logs' geometry plus about 6 never-deleted instance buffers |

  - The heap stayed flat (precise memory info).
- **Cause.**
  - `veg.single()` (`vegetation.js:530-533`) makes one or two `InstancedMesh` per tree.
  - `occlusion.js:111` (`scene.remove(e.single)`) and `harvest.js:279` (`scene.remove(f.pivot)`) never call
    `InstancedMesh.dispose()`, so each `instanceMatrix` GL buffer stays alive after the mesh is garbage-collected.
  - The fader makes these every time a trunk comes between the camera and the hero.
- **Fix.** Call `dispose()` on the single's meshes (not their shared geometry or materials) in `restore` and after the
  fall. Or pool the singles per species and variant.
- **Gain.** No GPU buffer growth on a long walk through the woods (hundreds an hour). Risk: none. **S.**
- **The playtest "leak" lead is mostly not a leak.**
  - `renderer.info.memory.geometries` and `textures` count what has been uploaded, not what exists. With the camera
    turning 1.3 rad a round and no actions at all, geometries went 470 → 628 and textures 96 → 127 while scene objects
    stayed at 1862: things already in the scene being drawn for the first time.
  - A 3-round tour of forest, temple, labyrinth, summit and meadow came back to identical counts after the first round:
    767 geometries, 127 textures, 2268 objects and 462 MB heap in rounds 1–3 (`leak.mjs`).
  - The bot's growth of 410 → 1231 geometries and 91 objects a minute is the first upload of everything it travelled
    to, plus the logs, drops and buildings it left behind. Logs are never cleared until split: each keeps a cloned
    geometry, by design, but this is unbounded over a long game.

### 10. The heap is 450 MB, nearly all of it typed arrays, and 50 MB of that is raw model files kept after parsing
- **Evidence.**
  - `performance.memory` without `--enable-precise-memory-info` is bucketed: the suite's 462, 491 and 521 MB are its
    buckets. With the flag, the heap is 443–453 MB after load (load 2.8–3.0).
  - A heap snapshot (`snapshot.mjs`/`snapsum.mjs`) shows 399 of 448 MB is `JSArrayBufferData`. By owner:

    | Owner | Size |
    |---|---|
    | the `buffers` Map in `assets.js:15`: every fetched .glb/.fbx kept as a Promise of its raw ArrayBuffer forever (the largest single one 16.8 MB) | 49.4 MB |
    | geometry `position`, `normal` and `uv` arrays (CPU copies of what is on the GPU) | 47.1, 47.1 and 26.4 MB |
    | instance matrices, of which the grass alone is 10.2 + 6.4 MB | 23.2 MB |
    | data textures (the 1024² height map and others) | 8.9 MB |

  - The scene's own geometry is 137 MB, 91 MB of it in the sites' groups.
  - The site pieces' source geometry stays in `PIECES` (site-pack) and in `templeCtx.P` after it has been copied into
    the placed meshes. For example, `plTemple`'s 2.7 MB position and 2.7 MB normal arrays exist twice.
- **Fix.**
  1. In `assets.js`, drop a buffer from `buffers` once it has been parsed, keeping only files parsed more than once
     (`hero_rig`), or cache the parsed result instead. This saves about 49 MB, risk low, S.
  2. Release `PIECES` and `templeCtx.P` geometry after the sites are built, unless a later rebuild needs them (ruins
     use their own groups). About 10–20 MB, M.
  3. Use the grass offset attribute from item 3: 16 MB.
  4. Optionally, for static site meshes that nothing ray-tests, `attr.onUpload(() => attr.array = null)` would drop
     their CPU copies. This is risky because colliders, aiming and the fader use some of them.
- **Gain.** About 80–100 MB less heap and shorter major GCs. Risk: low for (1) and (3). **S/M.**

### 11. The atmosphere pass in the woods (the woods' haze march) costs about 1.7 ms on High; Ultra marches 64 steps at half resolution
- **Evidence.** Atmosphere off: render 3.64 → 1.93 ms and 49.9 → 52.4 fps in the forest (load about 5; the effect is
  only in woods, because `woodK` is 0 in meadows).
- **Cause.** `atmosphere.js:343-350`: a half-resolution march with nine depth-weighted taps in the composite.
- **Fix.** On High, march at quarter resolution with the existing temporal blend (`hist`), or fewer steps where the
  wood's density texture is flat. Picture risk: a little softer haze edges.
- **Gain.** About 0.5–1 ms of GPU time in the forest on High. Risk: medium (`light.mjs` must still pass). **M.**

### 12. Ultra's cost against its benefit (for the RTX 5080 player)
- **Evidence.** Forest on Ultra: 621 draws and 6.97M triangles, against High's 508 and 3.40M (`gpu.mjs`, load 3.9).
  The triangles split as follows:

  | Part | Triangles |
  |---|---|
  | grass | 3.04M |
  | vegetation, main pass | 2.64M |
  | vegetation, far shadow map | 1.28M |
  | sites | 0.49M |

  On this Mac: grass off → 30.9 fps, vegetation off → 35.2 fps, resolution at 70% → 27.5 fps, against 22.9 fps with
  everything on. That points to the vertex and geometry work, not the pixels.
- **Poor value.** The sparse grass carpet out to `GRASS_END` 180 m, inside a mist that is 42% thick by 200 m: the far
  carpet is 100k × (360/190)² ≈ 359k blades. Halving the far carpet's density beyond 120 m would save about 1M
  triangles that nobody can see.
- **The rest is fine on a 5080.** The audit measured ±1 fps each for 8× MSAA, full-resolution AO and half-resolution
  shafts.
- **CPU per frame on Ultra is 7.9–9.2 ms**: `veg.cull` 1.5 ms (with item 6), occlusion 0.9 ms, and three's render 5 ms
  for 626 draws. On D3D11 that is the main risk at 60 Hz, so items 2, 4, 5 and 6 matter more there than here.
- **Effort.** S for the grass thinning, as a change of numbers in `reach.js` and `grass.js` that will need new
  baselines.

### 13. Smaller things (each S, all low risk)
- **The shelter maps' redraw** (`wetness.js:53`, every 2 s when dry and every 0.5 s in rain) draws the whole scene
  twice with one `overrideMaterial`. Instanced and plain meshes share it, so programs are looked up again on every draw
  that switches between them. The `shelter maps` lap peaked at 5.1 ms. Fix: two override depth materials, one
  instanced and one plain, or `customDepthMaterial`-style layers.
- **Three's shadow pass uses one depth material for skinned, morphed, instanced and plain casters,** so 3 look-ups a
  frame. The hero's `base_legs` (skin and morph) and `brows` look up their standard program once a frame each.
  Minor.
- **The terrain quadtree** (`terrain.js:57`, `:73`) builds new arrays and a `Set` every frame: 37 KB a frame. Reuse
  them.
- **`build.near()`** (`build.js:109`) is a generator with string keys, called several times a frame by `floorAt`,
  `underRoof` and `groundHere`: 13–15 KB a frame. Use number keys, as `gen.js` already does.
- **The HUD** (`hud.js:104-112`) writes 7 `style.width` values and the health text (`textContent`) every frame,
  whether or not they changed: 1–2 DOM writes a frame. Write only on change, as `stam` already does.
- **Audio.** `updateAmbience`, `fires` and the beds make about 21 `AudioParam` automation calls a frame (1,260 a
  second) whether or not the targets moved, and `game.js:178` passes `{ ...g.amb }` (a new object every frame). Skip
  the calls when the target hasn't moved by more than 1%.
- **Two atmosphere quads** (`atmosphere.js:364-365`: the shafts' mask and blur, the AO) compiled mid-play the first
  time the light called for them (seen under time ×60). `warmUp` only compiles the scene, so render the composer once
  at load with shafts and AO forced on.
- **`perf.mjs`'s heap figure is bucketed** (462, 491 or 521 MB). Add `--enable-precise-memory-info` to `GPU_ARGS`, or
  stop reporting it.

## Load and hitches after `warmUp`
- **Load** (load 3.0, `heap.mjs`): first `requestAnimationFrame` at 0.62 s, playable (hero in and terrain settled) at
  5.7 s. 155 programs were linked during load, and 146–148 live during play. The worker pool runs 4 workers on 10
  cores.
- **Mid-play compiles.** Standing or walking in the forest, the only compiles are the PMREM ones (item 1) and the two
  atmosphere quads (item 13). Felling a tree first time added one program.
- **Uploads per frame on High:** 36 KB in the meadow, 86–100 KB in the forest (walking included), 30 KB of texture
  data. On Ultra, 255 KB. These match the audit's post-fix levels.
- **GC:** 0.5 minor GCs a second at 1–2 ms each, and no major GC in 6 s. Allocation is 36–41 MB/s on High in the forest,
  up from the audit's 20 MB/s: items 2 and 8 account for about 21 MB/s.

## What I'd do first
1. Item 1 (one line, the regression).
2. Item 2 (one flag per material).
3. Items 8 and 9 (small and safe).
4. Items 3, 4 and 5: they bring the High forest back under the M4's GPU budget, and they cut the draws that cost most
   on D3D11 for the player's Ultra.
